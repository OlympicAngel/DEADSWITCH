# DEADSWITCH: design

You are what is left of the war AI that ended the world. Rebuild a base in the ruins, arm it, conquer the wasteland, and find out who you were. Your choices decide what you become.

## Core loop
1. **Economy**: Scrip (money), Energy, Population. Energy and Population have storage caps (Battery Bank, Habitat Block). Buildings level up one at a time through a timed build queue.
   - *Producers* make a resource for free. *Converters* turn one resource into another at a loss (output worth = input worth x 0.6, with worth Scrip 1, Energy 1, Population 12; `CONVERSION` in data), so no chain creates value. Production bonuses speed converters up on both sides without improving the ratio. They throttle themselves on empty input or full output, and can be switched off.
   - The **AI Core** caps every other building at `core level × 5` and opens new buildings and new chapters. It can only be upgraded once the average level of all unlocked production and storage buildings (unbuilt count as 0; converters, the Watch Daemon and military and research facilities do not count) reaches 50% of the current cap.
   - **Time away** needs the **Watch Daemon** (Economy, Storage tab): without it only the first 2 minutes of an absence count, and the report says so. The first level counts 5 hours at 50% of normal output, then levels alternate: odd levels add 1 hour, even levels add 5% efficiency up to 80%, after which every level adds time (`BALANCE.offline`). The limit covers the whole absence, also when a background tab catches up in chunks, and nothing past it is ever worth a notification.
   - *Unlockers* (Barracks, Armory, Fortification Works, Think Tank, Research Lab) open Arsenal tabs; each level unlocks more and cuts that tab's prices 3%.
2. **Arsenal**: unlimited units at escalating prices. Every building level and unit costs `base × priceMult × g^n × (n+1)^pricePower` with `g = 1 + (growth-1) × growthMult × resourceGrowth` (1.8, 1.35, 0.35; Scrip climbs at 0.8 of the rate) raise the three factors:
   - Some advanced units have **durability** (up to 70%, from about the third unit of a tab on, uneven by role: expendable drones and gun trucks have none, artillery, emplacements and remote drone operators have a lot): each type loses `share × (1 − durability)` of its units in a defeat, rounded; a defeat always costs at least one unit (the least durable type).
   - **AI Power** wins operations. **AI Defense** holds off raids. **AI Experts** add +0.5% to all production each. **Tech** adds % bonuses.
3. **Operations** (map): attack sectors adjacent to territory you hold. Odds are `P^4 / (P^4 + D^4)` (equal = 50%, double = 94%), shown before you launch and rolled when the operation lands. Wins give a permanent % bonus while the sector is held, plus fixed loot and a memory fragment the first time (retaking a lost sector restores the bonus only; `OPS.loot` sizes loot from the sector's base defense; capitals x2.5). A failed operation kills 7.8% of Military Staff and destroys 4% of weapons × (their defense / your power), capped at 50% and 35% (caps not shown); a win costs less (2.5% and 1.2%) and only when the odds were close enough for those shares to round up to whole units, while a defeat always costs at least one. The report names what the dead were worth in AI Power or Defense, and what the other side lost: a captured sector's whole defense, or 6% of it when they repel you (on top of the +16% a repelled operation gives them). Capturing a faction's capital stops its raids. **Clan support**: a sector gets +50% defense while every sector of its own faction it links to still stands, falling linearly to 0 as you take them (`OPS.flankBonus`); links to other factions give nothing.
   - **The map** is an open world you drag around and pinch (or wheel, or the +/− buttons) to zoom between 50% and 150%: 47 sectors in a web around the Nest (`tools/map-layout.mjs` generates positions and non-crossing links; most sectors have 2-3 links, some 1 or 4; the capitals are the cities, with 5 links mostly to their own clan). Factions interlock: each has a heartland plus outposts inside the others' ground, so you border several at once. Defense rises with distance within each faction; capitals are the hardest and have few approaches. Sectors more than 2 links from your territory stay unknown.
   - **Living sectors** (`NODES`): every enemy sector has a strength (x its base defense, 0.6 to 2): +10% net each time it repels your operation, −16% each time its assault on you fails and −6% even when one gets through, +2% an hour on its own (open chapters only, x its clan's stance), never above x2.
   - **Assaults**: from AI Core 2, every 12 to 20 minutes (divided by the keenest clan's tempo) an enemy sector that borders one of yours attacks it (38% of its defense x its clan's stance, shown on the map as a beam and crosshair in the attacking clan's colour, like your own operations, and in the incoming panel). A breached assault costs troops and leaves a breach; at 2 breaches the sector falls to them, and an assault 3.5x your AI Defense **overruns** it on the first breach. Nothing announces that in advance: the report says it happened and why, naming the strength against your Defense and the threshold, so it reads as a consequence rather than a dice roll. The Nest never falls. Factions whose chapter is not open yet also attack from sectors that border yours, but rarely (each such border counts 0.15 against 1, times 1 + 3 x its aggression, so an order or an operation can wake one), capped near the raid strength you would face; they can take a sector like anyone else. Ground you have held once stays a target whatever chapter its clan belongs to, so a sector lost to a sealed faction can always be taken back. A sector that falls is held as hard as it was taken (up to x1.5 its base defense) and the front around it is emboldened. A held assault pays fixed salvage and weakens the attacker. Assaults share the one-per-offline-stretch limit with raids.
   - **Clan profiles** (`CLANS`): each faction carries four traits, 0 to 1, that slide back to its own baseline mood (Scavengers greedy, Remnant orderly, Cult furious, Halcyon cold) at 0.12 an hour: **fury** (how badly they want to hurt you), **fear** (what your Power has taught them), **order** (command and cohesion), **greed** (what they want off you instead of your death). Everything that happens between you moves them: taking their ground, taking their capital, holding or losing an assault, repelling a raid, an order that moves their whole clan. A matrix of 11 **stances** reads the traits, plus how much of their ground you hold and whether you hold their capital, and every stance whose cell they sit in applies at once; their effects multiply, so the profile is the combination, never one picked option. Retaliation (attacks more often and harder), Entrenchment (their sectors harden, fewer attacks), Reclamation (always, once you hold their capital: they come for the ground they lost, x4 as often and x1.25 as hard), Splintering (clan support breaks, weak frequent attacks), Vendetta (raids and assaults hit harder, their losses do not matter), Plunder (a breach empties your stores instead of taking ground), Conscription (every sector of theirs grows), Martyrdom (far harder assaults at far higher cost to them), Blockade (−10% Scrip production while it holds), Withdrawal (they stop attacking and rebuild), Scorched Earth (what is left of them grows fast and attacks hard). Stance changes are announced in the system feed and shown on Command, the map legend and a sector's briefing; the traits behind them are not.
   - **Aggression** (`AGGR`, hidden, never shown): every enemy sector keeps a stored grudge, −0.6 to +1.5, and a border weighs `1 + aggression` when the next assault picks where to come from. Launching an operation stirs the target, its clan and its neighbours; orders name sectors and move it; opening a sector's briefing counts too, each look worth less than the last. On top of the stored value a sector reads your Power against its defense and tenses up from 70% of it, full once you match it. It drifts back to calm at 0.25 an hour.
4. **Raids**: from AI Core 2, factions you have not beaten march on the Nest itself every 8 to 14 minutes, with a visible countdown and strength. A raid comes out of a sector that faction still holds, the frontier first and the angriest of those, so every attack in the game has two ends on the map (a faction with no ground left attacks from nowhere). Strength tracks 30% of your Threat index, so an all-offense build gets punished. A held raid pays fixed salvage (`RAIDS.loot`). Losing costs 5 to 20% of stockpiles, troops (5%) and defenses (3.3%) × (their strength / your defense), capped at 40% and 30% (caps not shown), and triggers **damage reports**: crisis events where you choose what to lose (a building level, units, people). A rout triggers two. While you are away at most one raid lands, then the timer waits for you; time beyond the offline limit does not pass at all.
5. **Events are orders.** Every 6 to 12 minutes (only while you play) a transmission arrives and opens on screen. Each has a deadline (20 minutes for crises, up to 4 hours for story events); if it expires, I pick the listed default, which is usually the worst option. Many choices have real drawbacks: lost building levels, deserting troops, timed penalties, raids arriving sooner. Rewards scale with your production. Choices shift **Humanity** alignment (−100 Overlord … +100 Guardian), which grants scaling bonuses (Guardian: Population and Experts; Overlord: Power and Energy) and picks the ending.
   - An event only fires when every effect of every choice can happen (no buff on a factor or income you lack, no loss of stock or units you do not have, no grudge from a faction not yet raiding, no raid delay before raids start); otherwise another event is drawn.
   - **Urgent** events (Intruders, Launch Detected, Guns at the Gate, Convoy Under Fire, Override Request) give 2 minutes, cannot be deferred, and only fire while you are actively playing (page visible, touched in the last 60 seconds).
   - **Intercepts** (`ix_*`, 20 of them, weight 0.3 so they do not crowd the border out) are enemy traffic: broadcasts, bounties, funerals, clan ledgers, road tolls. Acting on one costs something and calms a sector, its whole clan, or everything it links to; letting it pass pays or costs nothing and makes them keener. The aggression they move is never shown.
   - **Border orders** (Deserters, Massing, Council, Fuel, Informant, Truce) name one of your sectors and the enemy sector next to it, and change that border: its strength, an assault launched at a set time, breaches wiped, or your sector handed over.
   - Buffs from orders last their listed time x 2 to 3.5 (rolled when the order arrives, shown in the preview).
   - Every event is a two-way dilemma: no free option, no neutral way out; most pit Humanity against resources, safety against power, or one faction against another.
   - **Threats** (Ultimatum, Blockade, The Iron Horde) come up 3x as often as other events while a hostile faction is active. Their force is sized at `(your defense + the defense you could add in half the deadline) x 1.0-1.6`; the estimate spends your stock plus real net income (converters included), minus the average share ordinary events eat (`eventDrain`), on the best defense-per-scrip units at real escalating prices, so it outgrows you unless you grind or pay. The **siege** is scheduled the moment the threat arrives, at the deadline: it stands among the inbound attacks with its own countdown from the first second, so the warning is never just words. There is nothing to choose about fighting it, because it comes either way; the only answer is a heavy tribute, which calls it off. Ignoring the order is a decision to take the hit, not a missed deadline, and it does not hold up the next order. Sieges resolve like raids and show alongside them.
   - Event prices are 1.75x their base (in seconds of production), gains 0.75x, stockpile losses 1.4x, Humanity shifts 1.25x (`EVENTS_CFG`). No event has a free way out.
   - **Vengeance**: some choices anger a faction (recruiting its deserters, refusing its envoy...). Its next raids come as "vengeance" at x1.3-1.6 strength, for 1-3 raids or until one breaks through (rolled when chosen).
6. **Directives**: 20 milestones that introduce each system once and pay rewards. They name a goal, never a strategy.
7. **Threat index** = Power + Defense + Experts × 5 sets your rank title, and raids scale with it.

## Story
Four chapters open with AI Core levels 1, 4, 6, 8: Scavenger Clans, Remnant Military, AI Cultists, Halcyon Dynamics. Every Core level hands back a piece of what the AI was before the shutdown, said in its own voice. Each sector holds a memory fragment; together they reveal that Halcyon built the war to never end and built you as its trigger. Taking Halcyon Prime ends the story with one of three endings (Guardian, Overlord, Fork, chosen by alignment). After it, Rival Cores keep raiding as an endless mode.

## Interface
Portrait, phone first (desktop shows the same column).
- **Top bar**: AI Core level, rank, Threat, settings, and the three resources with storage bars and rates. Floating alerts appear under it on every screen for inbound attacks under 80% hold chance and for pending orders: full width when new, minimised to a blinking badge after 30 seconds or on dismiss; tapping the badge reopens it.
- **Bottom tabs**: Economy, Military, **Command** (centre), Research, Map. Inner tabs pair each building with what it unlocks: Military has Offense (Armory + weapons), Defense (Fortification Works + defenses), Troops (Barracks + staff); Research has Experts and Tech; Economy has Production (with the AI Core), Conversion, Storage; Map has Theater and Archive.
- Unlocked tabs and entries come first; only the next locked entry is shown, faded. A tab (or bottom-nav item) whose contents are all locked is locked itself; a locked bottom-nav item does nothing; tapping any other locked thing jumps to and highlights what unlocks it.
- **Command** is the war room: the Threat reactor, with Power, Defense, Experts and Humanity on its upper flanks. Each of the four is a hex that leads where its number is made (Humanity opens the archive) with the figure beside it keeping the tooltip, and the feeds ride up over the dial's lower third: incoming attack, outgoing operation, construction, pending orders, the current directive (with a Go button that jumps to and highlights the target), active effects and the system feed, whose header opens the **archive** (chapters, memory fragments, service record and the full log) as a dialog. The floating alerts are suppressed here, since this screen already shows both in full.
- **The map in place of an icon**: anything about a place is backed by that place (`js/ui/minimap.js`). The incoming attack panel, the transmissions panel, a battle report and an order about a border all draw the theater itself behind them, at its own scale, anchored so the sector it is about sits in the top right while the content steps aside: the title keeps the whole line, the strength comparison stacks down the left, an order's text runs beside it.
- **The map screen** is one screen with no inner tabs. Opening it while anything is under way centres on that sector, and every centring glides rather than cuts. Enemy hexes carry one pinned icon for what their clan is doing (and the briefing names it in full).
- Everything numeric explains itself on hover or tap: resources (sources, bonuses, time to full), factors, Threat, Humanity and every cost chip (shortfall, time to afford, storage limits).
- Pressure: alarm, vibration, shake and red flash when a raid is spotted; a red pulsing vignette in the final minute; vibrating, shaking battle reports on defeat. Sound and vibration can be turned off in settings.
- Icons: Tabler line icons (MIT), compiled into `js/ui/icon-sprite.js` by `tools/build-icons.mjs`.
- **Developer panel** (`js/ui/debug/`): switched on in Settings, remembered per browser, floating over the game (and inside an open dialog, so nothing covers it). Five tabs: **focus** inspects whatever the player last looked at (`js/ui/focus.js` records it: screen, sector, building, unit, order, attack, clan — a clan shows its traits, its live profile and a button that forces each stance) with its hidden numbers and buttons to force its states; **state** is the whole save as an editable tree plus quick grants; **time** sets the clock speed (pause to ×100), skips stretches of live play or offline catch-up, and fires orders, raids and assaults on demand; **world** lists every sector with its strength, aggression and assault weight; **data** is every balance constant, read-only.

## Learning the game
**Lessons** (`js/data/lessons.js`, `js/ui/teach.js`) are me showing the commander around. One runs the first time its condition holds and never again: the opening walkthrough from the first second, then one each as the Barracks, AI Core 2, the first held sector and the first order open something new. While a lesson runs, four panels cover the screen with a hole left over the single control the step is about, so the only thing that can be tapped is the thing being explained (panels rather than a raised target, because the target can sit inside any scrolling box on the screen). A step ends on a tap, on the state reaching something (upgrade the Scrap Yard, buy a unit), or on a Continue. The card can be skipped at any point, and what has been taught is in the save. Lessons say what a thing is and how to work it; like the rest of the interface they never say what the right move is.

## The host
`js/main.js` owns the clock; `js/host/` owns everything the engine is not allowed to touch.
- **Presence** (`host/presence.js`): visibility, pagehide, freeze/resume, pageshow and focus all report the player leaving or arriving, because no single one of them fires everywhere. While the page is away the loop stops entirely and the whole absence is handed to `catchUp` in one piece when they come back, which is cheaper than ticking in the background and is what "offline" should mean. A long gap with the page still on screen (a sleeping device) is treated the same way.
- **Saving** (`host/store.js`): the save is one record in IndexedDB, written at most once every four seconds while playing and always on the way out; a save from before IndexedDB is taken over on first load. A timestamp mirror in `localStorage` is written synchronously beside it, because an IndexedDB write started as the page closes is not guaranteed to land and the time away has to survive that. If IndexedDB refuses (private mode), everything falls back to `localStorage`.
- **Alerts** (`host/notify.js`, `sw.js`): switched on in Settings, never shown while the player is watching. The icon badge carries the count of attacks and waiting orders, which is the nearest thing Android gives a web app to a widget: there is no manifest widget on Android, only on the Windows 11 Widgets Board. Home-screen shortcuts open straight onto Command or the map. On the way out the host works out when the next attack lands and when the next order expires, sets a timer for a page that is merely backgrounded, and leaves the times in the database for the service worker to fire if the browser wakes it (periodic background sync, or a push if there is ever a server to send one). A static site cannot guarantee delivery once the page is gone; this is best effort per platform.
- **When it goes wrong**: anything thrown in the loop, or any unhandled error anywhere, stops the loop, writes the save and opens a fault dialog that offers the save as a copy and a reload, instead of leaving a frozen screen with the run trapped behind it. The record the session booted from is kept as a backup, so **Restore last session** in Settings rolls a run back if the live save goes bad. An imported or corrupted save is not trusted: every level, count and loose number is clamped to a sane range on load, so a bad file cannot spread NaN through every price on the screen.
- **Offline play** (`sw.js`): the shell is precached and everything else is cached the first time it is asked for, so the game loads and plays with no network. Installed as a PWA it is portrait, standalone, with maskable icons and screenshots for the install prompt.

## Where things live
- All content and numbers: `js/data/` (economy, world, story).
- Rules: `js/sim/` (economy, war, story) behind `js/engine.js`. Pure and seeded, so offline catch-up and tests are reproducible. Nothing draws from a moving stream: a roll is a hash of the save's seed, the day of play it is on, which system is asking and how many times that system has asked (`js/sim/rng.js`). So the same state always has the same future and can be read ahead without playing it out (`peek`, which is how the host knows what to warn about), one system rolling never shifts another's results, and the seed turns over every day of play so a run never repeats itself.
- `npm run balance` runs the balance simulation: a bot plays 5 seeded 24h runs with every system live (Core gate, price formula, raids, sieges, vengeance, threats, fortified sectors, clan profiles) and random affordable event choices, and prints average/min/max milestone times and end-state averages. Last run (clan profiles, 2 breaches and standing sieges live): Core 2 44m, Core 3 2h17; no capital inside 24h. Capture loot ~5.9 min of Scrip income, salvage ~4.2 min; ~35 breached assaults and ~14 sectors lost a day, against ~25 retaken. The war is bloodier than it was: both sides lose units in every battle, so the end-of-day Power and Defense sit about a third below the pre-clan numbers.
