# Playbook: Player engagement and fairness

Use for game ideas, mechanics, progression loops, UI flows, balance changes, and player-facing implementation. This playbook supplements (and never overrides) `docs/design/10_resolved_decisions.md`.

## Evaluate the experience

1. **Player value:** What player goal or fantasy does this serve, and which ranked design pillar does it support?
2. **Enjoyment:** What does the player choose or do, what makes the result satisfying, and what feedback communicates it?
3. **Forward pull:** Is there a meaningful next decision or visible progress that makes another session appealing? Create earned momentum through agency, mastery, discovery, anticipation, or relief—not obligation.
4. **Function and fairness:** Are costs, state changes, outcomes, and failure understandable? Check counterplay, recovery, offline outcomes, mobile usability, and accessibility against canon.
5. **Validation:** Choose a proportionate test, such as comprehension questions, task completion, or playtest feedback. Treat engagement as a hypothesis, not a proven outcome.

## Boundaries

- Keep pressure legible, bounded, and recoverable. Losses need recognizable causes, counterplay, and a recovery path under the resolved rules.
- Never rely on deceptive urgency, shame, punitive absence, distressing notification pressure, manipulative reward schedules, or pay-to-win pressure.
- Preserve opt-in notifications, accessibility, effect-intensity controls, release scope, and the resolved monetization rules.
- Improve clearly beneficial, low-risk details within scope and canon. Ask before subjective or material changes to scope, difficulty, economy, rewards, losses, timers, monetization, notifications, accessibility, or intended player emotion.
- Do not optimize time spent, check-in frequency, or retention in isolation. Players should be able to stop at a satisfying point and return without penalty or shame.

For substantial work, state the player value, source of enjoyment, fairness risks/system interactions, and how the result can be validated.
